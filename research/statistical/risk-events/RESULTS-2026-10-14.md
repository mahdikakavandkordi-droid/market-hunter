# Risk-event probability v1 — exploratory results, 14 October 2026

## Assessment

This new target produces a limited research candidate, not a general crisis warning system. Historical volatility state predicts an asset-relative future high-volatility event substantially better than unconditional event frequencies in most cells. The harder incremental question is mixed: adding acceleration, current-shock magnitude and recent downside to calibrated current volatility improves the overall h10 Brier score for S&P 500 by 4.94% before 2020 and 14.64% in the later vintage. Only the latter overall comparison survives the predeclared 36-test Holm family (adjusted p=.036). No calm-origin comparison survives correction.

The narrow candidate worth retaining is S&P 500 h10 probability forecasting. The evidence does not justify promoting the full model across all nine markets or claiming reliable advance warning from calm conditions. No portfolio strategy, future collection schedule, live signal or production integration was activated.

## Hypothesis, target and precommitment

At each origin t, define q(t) as the 80th percentile of the most recent 252 trailing-20-bar RMS volatility observations. The event is future RMS over 5/10/20 native bars exceeding the origin's fixed q. The threshold is causal and never revised with future outcomes. A calm origin means current v20<=q, so its positive future event is the defined onset diagnostic. This is a relative recent-volatility event, not a crash, return direction, universal economic danger threshold or realized investment loss. The 80th-percentile definition does not guarantee a 20% future event frequency.

The four-feature logistic model uses log(v20/q), log(v5/v20), scaled last absolute return and scaled negative 20-bar return. All settings were fixed in `PROTOCOL-2026-10-14.md`: lambda20, training-only scaling, 252 matured targets and 21-bar refits. Strong comparator: one-feature logistic on log(v20/q), fitted on identical dates and labels. Other baselines are matured historical event rates, high/calm state-conditional rates, idealized EWMA(.94) Gaussian chi-square event probabilities and a hard current-high rule.

## Advance-warning example and false alarms

At the predeclared .25 warning cutoff, later S&P calm origins contain 136 positive target observations out of 871 origins, in 21 contiguous sampled event episodes. The full model warns on 186 observations: 67 true positives and 119 false positives, misses 69 positives, precision 36.0%, recall 49.3%, false-positive rate 16.2%. The level-only baseline warns 240 times: 62 true positives, 178 false positives, recall 45.6%, false-positive rate 24.2%.

This is descriptively better warning quality at that fixed cutoff, but nearly two-thirds of warnings are false and about half of positive observations are missed. Observations and alerts overlap; these counts are not independent warnings or crisis events. The calm-origin corrected test remains nonsignificant. All .25/.50/.75 cutoffs were specified in advance; none is selected as a trading policy.

## Calibration and sample limitations

Later S&P's <.20 probability bin has mean forecast .0995 and realized event frequency .1010 (614 overlapping observations), but its .20–.40 bin forecasts .2883 against realized .1792 (279 observations). Hence a lower Brier score does not imply uniformly calibrated probabilities. Bins are fixed, not optimized after evaluation; no fitted recalibration was applied.

The threshold-history warmup plus matured training labels delays later equity h10 evaluation until February 2022 and crypto until June 2021. It does not independently test the March 2020 crash. The older equity evaluation begins February 2007 and includes 2008; silver begins June 2008. ETH's older evaluation starts April 2019 and includes only 241 overlapping h10 forecasts, 34 positives and a short provider history. Long input-date ranges must not be confused with the scored forecast range.

## Inference and correctness

Primary family: 9 markets x 2 vintages x 2 populations (all/calm) at h10, 36 paired full-versus-level Brier comparisons, Holm. Circular block bootstrap uses 999 replications, fixed seed20261014, block20 with sensitivity10/40. It assumes approximate stationarity; changing regimes and dependent cross-asset observations limit interpretation. The family handles these declared tests, not every prior research decision. Other horizons and metrics are exploratory diagnostics. No synthetic test fixture is historical market evidence.

Synthetic tests passed for origin thresholds, target maturity, training normalization/fits and complete forecasts under future-price perturbations, probability bounds, single-class fallback, and calm-origin weighting on the uncompressed native axis. Independent SciPy chi-square survival checks passed 27 cases with maximum absolute error about 1e-15. Original source-bundle file hashes were verified before consuming any bars. All 18 asset/vintage groups completed with zero errors; nonfallback penalized-gradient checks remained below 1e-4 (maximum about1.90e-10).

Exact offline replay reproduced all period metrics and the Holm family. All 36 per-group forecast-ledger and fit-file SHA256 hashes match. Input-bar bytes, parent manifest hashes, source snapshots, dynamic thresholds, fitted probabilities and label maturity are preserved privately. Source bundles remain unchanged. The existing statistical, Market Hunter and SMC engines are not modified by this new module.

## Research disposition

Retain the S&P h10 full model as a narrow exploratory candidate alongside the simpler calibrated level model. Do not claim cross-asset superiority or reliable calm-to-high warning. The next scientifically appropriate validation would be a separately frozen, genuinely future comparison with predefined scoring, timing, duration and missing-data rules. Both historical vintages are now consumed for this target; any further tuning on them remains exploratory. No future collection was enabled by this implementation.
